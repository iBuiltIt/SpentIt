# Understand your spending

You remember the groceries, a few meals out, and that bill you paid.
But looking at what’s left at the end of the month, you might still
wonder: where did it all go?

SpentIt helps you understand where your money goes. You bring in your
transactions, organize them into categories that make sense to you,
and explore where your money went.

Perhaps you want to understand why this month cost more than last
month. Perhaps you’re simply curious about how much you spend on
groceries. SpentIt can help you make sense of it all.

## Making sense of everyday spending

Let’s follow a grocery purchase through a credit card statement.
There are three entries, but how much did you actually spend?

### You buy some groceries

On 2026-01-15, you spend **42.18** at Market using your credit card.

That purchase is a **transaction**: one entry in your account.
Giving it the category **Groceries** lets you find it alongside
your other grocery purchases, whichever account you paid from.

### You get a refund

On 2026-01-20, Market refunds **6.00** to your card.

With both the purchase and the refund in Groceries, your spending
for these groceries comes to **36.18**.

### You pay the credit card

On 2026-01-28, you pay **36.18** from your Chequing account to
the credit card.

You’ve already counted the groceries. Paying the card moves money
between your own accounts; it doesn’t mean you bought them again.

In SpentIt, you record that payment as a **transfer**. Reports leave
transfers out of spending, so the groceries count just once.

Three entries, and a grocery cost of **36.18**. Understanding how
those entries fit together helps you make sense of the total.

*Dates in this guide use year-month-day. Amounts use a comma for
thousands and a dot for cents, as in 1,234.56. Your own file follows
your chosen date and amount settings. This example uses one currency.*

## Start with your own accounts

Your SpentIt **file** keeps your accounts and transactions together.
An account can represent a bank account, a credit card, or cash.

Create a file once and add accounts yourself, or create the accounts
as part of an import.

There are two ways to add transactions:

- **Import…** brings them in from a file you’ve downloaded from
  your bank or credit-card provider.
- **+ New transaction** lets you enter one yourself.

Once you have some transactions, take a look through them. Do you
recognize the purchases? Is there anything that needs correcting?
If the same purchase came in twice, maybe from a second import,
those two entries need reviewing.

A transfer between your accounts is different: it belongs in both
accounts, as the card payment did in our example.

## Give the spending some meaning

Categories help you answer questions about your money. Groceries,
Eating out, and Transport bring related purchases together so you
can see what they add up to.

Start with something you’re curious about. If it’s groceries, find
those purchases and give them the Groceries category. Remember to
include any grocery refunds too.

A **report** then brings those amounts together for a period you
choose. You can see the total and look at the transactions behind it.

## Take it at your own pace

You don’t have to finish reviewing everything in one sitting.
Transactions you haven’t accepted yet stay in your file and already
count in your balances and reports.

When the transactions look right, use **Accept transactions**.

For now, a useful first step is to bring in some transactions and get
familiar with what’s there. You will be on your way to finding out
"Where did my money go?"
